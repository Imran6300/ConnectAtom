Summary
This playbook turns your working temperature-monitoring system into a product family. It covers three things:
which 20 ready-to-use products to build and in what order, how to build one reusable software platform (with
authentication and multi-customer separation) so each new product costs days instead of months, and how to
turn one-time hardware sales into monthly recurring revenue.

The core idea
• One platform, many products. A product is mostly a hardware variant plus a configuration file (channels,
  default alert rules, dashboard layout, report template). The platform stays the same.
• Hardware is the door-opener, subscription is the business. The customer pays once for the device
  and then a monthly fee per device for the dashboard, alerts, reports and support.
• Launch four products as one. Products 1-4 (cold room, medical fridge, restaurant freezer, godown) use
  the same hardware and the same software. Only the sales page, report format and default rules differ.
• Add products from customer demand. Build the next product only after at least two paying customers
  ask for it.

2. Product playbooks
Each card gives the buyer, the problem, the hardware, the software on top of the shared platform, price, how to

 1. Cold Room / Cold Storage Monitor
 Effort: Low | Hardware: ₹5–8k | Monthly: ₹400 | Est. cost: ₹2.0–3.2k

 Buyer              Cold storages, fruit and vegetable traders, ice-cream distributors, meat and fish wholesalers.
 Problem            A compressor trip or a door left open overnight spoils stock, and the owner finds out in the morning.
 Hardware           ESP32 gateway in an IP65 box mounted outside the room, 1-2 external probes rated to -30°C (PT100 or
                    TMP117 probe), mains adapter plus Li-ion backup so a power cut still sends an alert, optional buzzer.
 Software           Out-of-range-for-N-minutes rules, defrost-cycle mute window, power-cut and offline alerts, daily
 features           min/max/average summary on WhatsApp, multi-room dashboard.
 How to start       Install in 2 friendly cold rooms free for 30 days. Record how many alarms fire and what one spoiled
                    batch costs them. That becomes your first case study.
 Watch out          Wi-Fi inside metal rooms is weak: put the gateway outside or offer a 4G variant. Batteries lose capacity
                    in the cold, so keep them outside the cold zone.


 2. Vaccine / Medical Fridge Monitor with Compliance Reports
 Effort: Low | Hardware: ₹5–9k | Monthly: ₹500 | Est. cost: ₹2.5–3.8k

 Buyer              Pharmacies, clinics, diagnostic labs, blood banks, hospital wards.
 Problem            Medicines and vaccines must stay within 2-8°C, and auditors and insurers ask for continuous
                    temperature logs.
 Hardware           TMP117-class probe (about ±0.1°C) in a glycol or thermal-buffer bottle so door openings do not cause
                    false alarms, backup battery, optional small display.
 Software           2-8°C preset rules, excursion log (start, end, duration, peak), acknowledge-with-reason, PDF reports
 features           (daily, weekly, monthly) with timestamps, 1-3 year data retention, calibration certificate upload per
                    probe.
 How to start       Visit 10 pharmacies, ask to see their current paper log, offer to digitise it free for one fridge, and ask
                    which report format their auditor expects.
 Watch out          Sell 'audit-ready logs', not 'certified' or 'compliant', until you have verified the exact standards your
                    buyers need. Use probes that come with calibration certificates.


 3. Restaurant Walk-in Freezer / Food-Safety Logger
 Effort: Low | Hardware: ₹4–7k | Monthly: ₹300 | Est. cost: ₹1.8–3.0k

 Buyer              Restaurants, cloud kitchens, bakeries, caterers, hotel kitchens.
 Problem            Clipboard temperature logs get skipped or filled in later, and one failed freezer ruins thousands of
                    rupees of stock.
 Hardware           Same gateway as product 1, probes for freezer and chiller, optional second probe for hot-hold or
                    bain-marie.
 Software           Automatic logs every 15-30 minutes, shift-wise summary, alerts to kitchen manager and owner,
 features           mobile-first view, multi-outlet dashboard for chains.
 How to start       Target cloud-kitchen clusters where many kitchens share one building. One live dashboard demo on
                    your phone is the whole pitch.
 Watch out          Kitchens are hot, wet and greasy. Enclosure rating and cable strain relief matter more than extra
                    features.

3. The reusable software platform
Goal: build one multi-customer platform once, then add each new product as a configuration plus a firmware
profile. It matches your existing stack (Next.js, Node/Express, TypeScript, MongoDB, Redis/BullMQ, Clerk,
Razorpay), so you are not learning new tools while selling.

Devices publish readings to the broker. The ingest service checks them, drops duplicates and stores them. The rules engine
watches the stream and raises incidents. The API serves the dashboard and reports. Billing decides which features each

3.3 Data model
Every document carries a tenantId (the customer organisation). The tenant id is taken from the verified
session, never from the request body.

3.4 Authentication, authorisation and security
Three separate identities need authentication: people, devices, and programs (customer integrations).

People (dashboard users)
• Use Clerk Organizations: one organisation per customer, members with roles, invitations by email or
  phone. Add phone OTP login because many owners and supervisors prefer it to passwords. Offer optional
  two-factor for owner and admin roles.
• Store the Clerk organisation id on the tenant. Middleware resolves session, then membership, then tenantId,
  then role. Nothing downstream reads a tenant id from the client.
• Put a single function in the data layer that every query goes through and that always adds { tenantId }.
  Write automated tests that try to read another tenant's device, readings, reports and files and must fail.

Devices
• Unique identity per device. At manufacture, generate a device id and a random secret (or a client

• Broker authentication through your API. The broker calls an auth hook on connect: valid, claimed, not

• Topic permissions. A device may publish only to its own topics and subscribe only to its own command
    topic. Key topics by deviceId, not by tenant, so a device can be moved between customers without
    reflashing.

    instead of storing it under a tenant.
• Signed firmware. Verify the signature before applying an OTA image. On ESP32, plan for secure boot and
    flash encryption on production units.
• Revoke and rotate. The admin console can revoke a device, rotate its secret, or force a re-claim.
Programs (customer integrations)
• Scoped API keys per tenant (read-only readings, webhook subscriptions). Show the key once, store only a
    hash, allow rotation, and log use.
• Outgoing webhooks for incidents, signed with a per-tenant secret.
Platform hygiene
• Zod validation on every input, secrets in environment variables or a secret manager, daily database backups
    with a tested restore.
• Audit log for rule edits, user changes, billing changes, exports and every support impersonation by you.
• Privacy: a plain privacy policy, data-retention rules, and a way to export or delete a tenant's data. Review
    your obligations under India's Digital Personal Data Protection rules with a lawyer; keep personal data
    minimal (name, phone, email).
• Terms of service: describe the product as a monitoring aid, state what you do and do not guarantee, limit
    liability, and have a lawyer review it once. Stock loss from a missed alert is the biggest legal risk of this

3.6 Firmware (one codebase, product profiles)
• One ESP32 firmware (ESP-IDF or Arduino/PlatformIO) with pluggable sensor drivers selected by a product
  profile.
• Wi-Fi setup by BLE or a captive portal (access point named after the device), plus a factory-reset button.
• Offline ring buffer in flash (LittleFS or NVS) holding hours to days of readings, uploaded in order when the
  link returns.
• Local alarm on the device (buzzer and LED) using thresholds synced from the cloud, so it still alarms when
  the network is down.
• Watchdog and safe reboot, brown-out handling, and a heartbeat so the server can tell 'device dead' from
  'sensor reads fine'.
• OTA with dual partitions, signature check, and automatic rollback if the new version cannot connect.
• Remote config over the command topic: sampling interval, thresholds, calibration offsets, buzzer on/off.

3.7 Alerts and notifications
The alert engine is the heart of the product. A missed alert is the failure customers will remember, so build it
first and test it hardest.

Step                  What happens

1. Evaluate           On every reading, check the rules. Keep the state (for example 'first breach at 02:14') in Redis so
                      'above 8°C for 10 minutes' works without scanning the database.

2. Open incident      When a rule has been breached for its duration, open an incident once. Do not send a new alert for
                      every reading.

3. Notify level 1     Send WhatsApp (and push) to the first contact. Critical alerts also SMS.

4. Escalate           If nobody acknowledges within the delay (for example 10 minutes), call or message level 2, then
                      level 3. A delayed BullMQ job does this and the acknowledgement cancels it.

5. Acknowledge        A tap or reply records who and when, with an optional reason (for example 'defrost' or 'door open for
                      loading').

6. Resolve            Close the incident when readings return inside the range, using hysteresis so a reading hovering at
                      the limit does not flap. Send a 'back to normal' message.

Offline check         A job every minute marks devices offline after N missed intervals and raises an offline incident.

Quiet hours           Allowed for warnings, never for critical alerts.

Self-test             A weekly test message so contacts know the channel still works, and so you notice a broken
                      template early.

• Start the approvals in week 1. WhatsApp Business message templates need approval, and commercial
  SMS in India needs DLT registration. Both can take days to weeks. Voice calls need a provider account.
• Monitor your own system. Add an uptime check on the broker and API, and a 'dead-man' alert if no
  readings arrive from any device for 5 minutes. If your ingest breaks, you must know before your customers
  do.

4.6 Growth levers after the first customers
